## SECTION IX. APPELLEE'S DAMAGES REMAIN UNVERIFIED AND INADMISSIBLE

**Standard of Review:** De novo (summary judgment evidentiary sufficiency).

Appellee's damages claim fails the evidentiary requirements of Florida's amended Rule 1.510.

### A. The Evidentiary Record

Appellants' trial court filings identified a critical evidentiary gap. In their Reply to Plaintiff's Opposition to Motion for Summary Judgment (R. ___), Appellants established that Appellee's damages allegations "rely on nothing more than unpaid estimates for a washer dryer and unsupported invoices for the alleged remediation, offering no proof of actual payment." (R. ___, Def. Reply p.3).

More significantly, Appellants noted that Appellee's invoice "cannot override Miami Mold's subpoenaed contemporaneous records (Def. Motion Ex. 11), which confirms 'no proof of payment exists.'" (R. ___, Def. Reply p.3).

This is dispositive. Appellee sought summary judgment on damages without verified proof that any remediation expenses were actually paid. Under Florida's amended Rule 1.510, adopting the federal *Celotex* standard, "[s]peculative, estimated damages do not satisfy Rule 1.510 and cannot create a triable issue." (R. ___, Def. Reply p.3).

### B. Appellee Cannot Prove Damages by Self-Serving Declaration

Appellee's response to this evidentiary void was to submit unverified invoices and declarations. But invoices are not proof of payment. A declaration stating damages were incurred does not establish they were paid.

The distinction matters because:
1. Florida law requires proof of actual damages, not estimates
2. Third-party subpoenaed records (Miami Mold) showed no payment verification
3. Appellee bears the burden on damages; Appellants need not disprove speculative claims

### C. The Trial Court Erred by Awarding Unverified Damages

The Summary Final Judgment awarded damages without addressing this evidentiary deficiency. The trial court adopted Appellee's proposed findings verbatim—including damages calculations unsupported by proof of payment.

This Court reviews de novo whether the record evidence supports summary judgment. *In re Amendments to Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.510*, 317 So. 3d 72 (Fla. 2021). Where Appellee's own subpoenaed records confirm "no proof of payment exists," summary judgment on damages was improper.

## SECTION X. SPOLIATION MANDATES ADVERSE INFERENCE AGAINST APPELLEE

**Standard of Review:** De novo (application of spoliation doctrine).

### A. The Spoliation

Appellee unilaterally removed and destroyed property that was the subject of this dispute.

As Appellants documented at trial court: "Police Report #2019-00042552 documents Plaintiff's unauthorized removal and disposal of Defendant's property." (R. ___, Def. Reply p.2). The washer/dryer unit—which Appellee now claims was damaged—"was under manufacturer warranty—it could have been repaired rather than destroyed." (R. ___, Def. Reply p.2).

Instead of preserving evidence for inspection, Appellee disposed of it. This is textbook spoliation.

### B. *Valcin* Requires Adverse Inference

Under *Public Health Trust v. Valcin*, 507 So. 2d 596, 597 (Fla. 1987), "where relevant evidence is within the control of a party in litigation, and that party fails to produce it without satisfactory explanation, an inference may be drawn that such evidence would be unfavorable to that party."

The rule applies with full force here:
- The appliances were within Appellee's control
- Appellee disposed of them unilaterally
- No satisfactory explanation was provided
- The missing evidence goes directly to damages causation

### C. Appellee Cannot Manufacture a Factual Dispute Through Spoliation

Appellee's strategy appears to be: destroy the evidence, then claim a factual dispute exists. *Valcin* forecloses this tactic.

The proper remedy is an adverse inference instruction—or, at summary judgment, the inference operates as a matter of law. The Court must presume the destroyed evidence was unfavorable to Appellee.

This means:
1. The appliances were not damaged by Appellants (or damage was de minimis)
2. Appellee's disposal was deliberate concealment
3. The claimed damages cannot be substantiated

### D. The Trial Court Ignored Spoliation

The Summary Final Judgment makes no mention of spoliation, *Valcin*, or the adverse inference doctrine. The trial court awarded damages for the very items Appellee destroyed—without addressing Appellants' documented spoliation argument.

This was error. A judgment awarding damages for items whose condition cannot be verified—because the claimant destroyed them—cannot stand.
